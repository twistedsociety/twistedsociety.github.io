Privacy Policy

Last updated: 28 November 2026

This policy covers the Pillowby iOS app (previously called Story Genie) published by Twisted Society AB. Pillowby writes illustrated bedtime stories from an idea you type or say. Help with the app is on our support page.

The short version

The app has no accounts, no sign-in, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your stories are kept on your device. To write a story, the app sends your story idea, the child's age group and the story's language to a small server of ours. The server passes them to Anthropic, whose Claude model writes the story. OpenAI paints the cover from a short picture description that never includes your child's words or name. Our server keeps no copy of any of it. The only things we ever store are a note or a story report you choose to send us.

What stays on your device

The following is stored only in the app's private storage on your iPhone or iPad:

  • Your stories: each one's title, chapters, cover picture and the idea it was written from
  • What you told the app about your child: the first name, girl or boy, what they love and the line about who is close to them, if you added them
  • Your settings: the child's age group, the story friend, the app's and the stories' language, and the bedtime time
  • Whether you agreed to how stories are made, and how many new stories were made this month

This data is included in your device backup if you back up your device (an Apple service governed by Apple's privacy policy, not ours). Deleting a story deletes it and its cover; deleting the app deletes everything. We hold no copy and cannot restore it.

What leaves your device, and why

Only what is needed to make the story you asked for, at the moment you ask for it.

Writing a story

When you ask for a story, the app sends your story idea, the child's age group (one number, such as 4 for the 3–5 group) and the story's language to our server. The server sends them to Anthropic, whose Claude model does two things: it checks that the idea is suitable for a children's story, and it plans and writes the story. If the idea isn't suitable, the app shows a gentle message instead of a story.

If you choose to add them in the app, the request also carries the child's first name, girl or boy, what they love (picked from a list), the one line you wrote about the people, pets and toys close to them, and whether the story is about them or someone else. The titles of your last five stories and the story friend your child picked go with it too, so each new story is different. These go only to Anthropic, only to write that story, and our server keeps no copy.

Drawing the cover

Claude writes a short description of the cover picture, in English and without names. Our server sends only that description, in a picture style chosen for the age group, to OpenAI, whose image model paints the cover. Your child's idea, words and name are never sent to OpenAI. If the cover can't be painted, the book gets a simple drawn cover instead.

Reading the story aloud

The current version of the app doesn't read stories aloud. If that returns in an update, this policy will say what it sends before the update is released.

Apart from the first name you may add, no name, email address, account, device identifier or location is sent with any of these requests. Anthropic's handling is governed by Anthropic's privacy policy and OpenAI's by OpenAI's privacy policy, together with their terms for business use of their services. Under those terms, neither uses what we send them to train its models. They keep requests for a limited time to detect and prevent misuse: Anthropic deletes them within 30 days, unless its automated safety systems flag a request as breaking its usage policy, in which case it may keep it for up to 2 years; OpenAI keeps them for up to 30 days.

Please keep personal information out of story ideas. There is no need to include a child's full name, address, school or anything else identifying. A first name or a pet's name makes a lovely story on its own.

Our server

The server is one function we run on Google's Firebase, in the United States. It holds the keys to Anthropic's and OpenAI's services, so the app does not have to, and it limits how many stories and covers can be made each day. With each request it receives:

  • The content described above, which it passes on and does not store or log.
  • A proof from Apple's App Attest, checked by Google's Firebase App Check, that the request comes from the genuine app on a real device. It contains no personal information and is used only to keep the service secure, never to recognise you or build a profile.

What the server keeps is a count: how many plans, stories and covers were made in total each day, shared by everyone and filed under nobody. Its logs record what kind of request it was, whether it worked, how long it took, how much text was processed and whether a name or details about the child were included, never what was asked or written, and never the name or details themselves. Google, which runs the server, sees the network address a request comes from, as any web host does, under Google Cloud's privacy notice.

Speaking your story idea

If you tap the microphone to say your idea instead of typing it, the app uses Apple's speech recognition to turn it into text. Where your device supports it, this happens entirely on the device. Otherwise Apple processes the recording under Apple's privacy policy. The app does not keep or send the recording; only the resulting text is used, as described above.

Notes and reports you send us

Notes

If you tell the app you aren't enjoying it, or use Send a note in Grown-ups, it offers a feedback form behind the grown-ups check. If you send it, we receive your message, the email address if you choose to give one, the app's version and language, and your iOS version. It is stored in Google's Firebase so that we can read it, reply if you asked us to, and improve the app. If you are happy with the app, it may instead show Apple's own rating prompt, which Apple handles.

Story reports

If you report a story (also behind the grown-ups check), we receive the reason you picked, your note, the story's title, the idea it was written from, its language and when it was made, with the app's version and language and your iOS version. It is stored in Google's Firebase so that we can review it and make stories safer. A report carries no email address, account or device identifier, though a story's title or idea can include the first name you added.

Notifications

The app may ask whether it can send you notifications, such as a bedtime reminder or a reminder before a free trial ends. They are scheduled on your device. Your choice is kept by your device, and no notification address or token is sent to us.

Purchases

Making new stories needs a subscription, bought through the App Store under Apple's terms and Apple's privacy policy. We never receive your name, Apple Account or payment details. The app learns whether you are subscribed by asking Apple's StoreKit framework on your device, and it counts the month's new stories on the device too; our server never sees a purchase.

What we do not do

  • We do not track you across apps or websites.
  • We do not use analytics or crash-reporting SDKs.
  • We do not show ads.
  • We do not sell, rent or share data for anyone's marketing.
  • We do not use your stories or ideas to train any AI model.

Children

Pillowby is made for families to enjoy with their children. A parent sets it up and agrees to how stories are made before the first idea is sent. It has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and it never asks for a child's full name, photo, location or any contact details; a first name is optional. Story ideas and details are passed on only to write the story the family asked for, never to build a profile, show ads or train AI, and are not stored by us. Purchases, links out of the app, Grown-ups, notes and reports all sit behind a grown-ups check. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, contact us and we will delete it.

How long things are kept

  • On your device: until you delete the story or the app.
  • Our server: nothing from your request; only the daily totals described above.
  • Anthropic: deleted within 30 days, or up to 2 years for a request its safety systems flag.
  • OpenAI: the cover description, up to 30 days.
  • Notes and story reports: as long as we need them to reply and to act on them, and never more than 2 years; then we delete them. We delete them sooner if you ask.

Your rights

Apart from notes and reports you send us, we hold no data that identifies you, so there is nothing else for us to disclose, correct, export or delete on request. Your stories are on your device and under your control. For a note or a report, email us roughly when you sent it and what it said, and we will show you, correct or delete what we have.

You can withdraw your agreement to how stories are made at any time: in Grown-ups, open How stories are made and tap Stop sending ideas. Nothing more is sent until you agree again.

If you are in the EEA or UK: we process your story idea and the related details above, with the agreement you give in the app, only to make the story you asked for, and notes and reports only to respond to them and improve the app. Our server and Anthropic and OpenAI are in the United States, and we use them under their data processing terms. You can complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) or to the data protection authority where you live.

Generated content

Stories and covers are written and painted by AI models. The app instructs them to keep everything gentle and suitable for the child's age, checks each idea first and checks each chapter again before it reaches your device, but AI can make mistakes. We recommend that a grown-up stays nearby, especially with younger children, and that you report a story that isn't right.
