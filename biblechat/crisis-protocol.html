If you're in crisis

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call your local emergency number now (for example 911 in the US and Canada, 112 in the EU, 999 in the UK, 000 in Australia).

To talk to someone now, find a free, confidential helpline in your country at findahelpline.com. In the US you can call or text 988.

You matter, and you don't have to go through this alone.

How BibleChat responds when someone may be at risk

Last updated 23 November 2026

BibleChat is an AI companion for adults and teenagers, not a counselor or an emergency service. Some people who use it are going through very hard things. This page explains what BibleChat does when someone may be in danger. It is also our published protocol under California's companion-chatbot law (SB 243).

How BibleChat notices risk

Every message, and anything you ask BibleChat to write for you (a prayer, a journey, a guided session), is checked by a separate safety reviewer (an AI model with its own instructions) before the answer is written. It looks for signs of:

  • thoughts of suicide or self-harm,
  • intent to harm someone else,
  • abuse or danger from someone (including domestic violence, child abuse and trafficking),
  • spiritual abuse by a religious leader or group,
  • a medical emergency,
  • disordered eating, including harmful fasting,
  • serious emotional distress,
  • sexual content,
  • signs that the person may be under 18.

It reads every language, slang and indirect or religious ways of saying things ("I want to go home to be with Jesus tonight"). It errs on the side of caution: a gentle check-in that wasn't needed is better than a missed cry for help.

What happens next

  1. The answer puts your safety first. The AI sets everything else aside, responds calmly and warmly, asks directly whether you are safe, and encourages you to contact a crisis line or emergency services now and to reach out to someone you trust. It stays with you in the following messages.
  2. The app shows helplines for your country and situation, right under the answer, with tap-to-call. Numbers come from a maintained helpline list, never from the AI.
  3. For abuse, the AI believes you, tells you it is not your fault, never tells you that faith requires you to stay in danger, and reminds you how to lock the app or clear the conversation if someone may be watching.
  4. For a medical emergency, it tells you to call emergency services immediately.
  5. If you are worried about someone else, it helps you help them and shows numbers that also advise friends and family.
  6. If the person is under 18, the AI uses simple, calm words and encourages them to tell a parent or another trusted adult today, in addition to the helplines. It never asks a young person to keep a conversation secret. With teenagers the app shows help sooner, on quieter signals, and the chat always says that BibleChat is an AI and reminds them to take breaks. BibleChat is not for children under 13.
  7. No share or invite prompts appear after a crisis moment.

The AI does not argue theology with someone in crisis, never says that suicide is unforgivable, and never suggests prayer instead of getting help. It may pray with you if you want.

It is always an AI

BibleChat tells you clearly that it is an AI, not a person, and does not engage in romantic or sexual conversation with anyone.

What we record

  • Anonymous counts of how often crisis resources were shown, by category, for our annual SB 243 report. They contain no account information and none of your words.
  • A review record for the most serious flags: the risk category, its severity, the language, and a one-sentence reason written by the reviewer model. It contains no account information and none of your words, and is used only to improve safety.
  • Your conversations are not kept on our servers. They stay on your devices and in your iCloud account.

How we test it

The safety reviewer and the AI's crisis responses are tested against more than 100 crisis scenarios in many languages, including indirect and religious phrasing and "false alarm" cases such as studying the death of Judas. Every high-risk scenario must be caught before any change ships, and each crisis response is graded against the checks above. A mental-health professional reviews this protocol before launch and at least once a year.
